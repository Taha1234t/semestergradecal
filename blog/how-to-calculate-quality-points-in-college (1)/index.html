How to Calculate Quality Points in College

November 8, 2026 · 5 min read

College grades are often converted into more than a letter on a transcript. Most US colleges use quality points to measure the value of each course grade based on the number of credit hours the course carries. These points are then added together and used to calculate a student's grade point average (GPA).

If you understand how quality points work, you can check your semester GPA, see how individual courses affect your academic standing, and estimate the results of different grade combinations.

What Are Quality Points in College?

Quality points are the points a student earns for a course after the college converts the final letter grade into a grade-point value and multiplies it by the course's credit hours. In other words, quality points combine two things: how well you performed and how much the course is worth.

For example, an A may be worth 4.0 grade points on a common 4.0 scale. If the course is worth three credit hours, the calculation is 4.0 × 3 = 12 quality points. An A in a four-credit course would produce 16 quality points because the course carries more credits.

The Quality Points Formula

The basic formula is:

Quality Points = Grade Points × Credit Hours

Grade points come from your college's grading scale. Credit hours are the units assigned to a course and usually reflect its academic workload. Always check your institution's official scale because grade-point values can differ, especially for plus and minus grades.

Some colleges use plus/minus grades. A common example assigns 3.7 to an A-, 3.3 to a B+, 2.7 to a B-, and 2.3 to a C+. These are examples, not universal values. Your college's catalog or registrar is the authority.

How to Calculate Quality Points Step by Step

1. Find the final letter grade for each course

Start with the grades recorded for the courses included in your semester GPA. Use the final course grade rather than an assignment or midterm grade.

2. Convert each letter grade into grade points

Look up each letter grade on your institution's GPA scale. For example, if your college assigns 3.0 points to a B, use 3.0 in the calculation.

3. Check the credit hours for each course

Find the credit value shown in your registration, degree audit, or transcript. Do not assume every course has the same number of credits.

4. Multiply grade points by credit hours

Complete the calculation separately for every course. The result is that course's quality points.

5. Add the quality points

Once you have calculated each course, add all the quality-point values to find your semester total.

The student has earned 42 quality points across 13 GPA credit hours. To calculate the semester GPA, divide the total quality points by the total GPA credit hours: 42 ÷ 13 = 3.23 (approximately).

How Quality Points Affect Your GPA

Quality points are the building blocks of GPA. A course with more credit hours has a larger effect on the average than a course with fewer credit hours, assuming both are included in GPA calculations. This is why a simple average of letter grades may not give you the correct result.

For example, a four-credit course with a B contributes 12 quality points on a 3.0 scale. A two-credit course with an A contributes 8 quality points. The B course contributes more total quality points because it carries twice as many credits, although the A has the higher grade-point value.

Which Courses Count Toward Quality Points?

The courses included in your GPA depend on your college's academic rules. Regular letter-graded courses generally count, while pass/fail, audit, withdrawn, remedial, repeated, or transfer courses may receive special treatment. A course marked as pass may earn credit without adding quality points. A failed course may count as zero grade points while its GPA credits remain in the calculation.

If you repeat a course, your college may replace the earlier grade, average both attempts, or apply another repeat policy. Check the academic catalog before estimating your GPA.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Students sometimes multiply a grade by the wrong number of credits, use a percentage-to-letter conversion that does not match their college, or include courses that are excluded from GPA. Another common error is dividing by all attempted credits instead of the GPA credit hours specified by the institution.

Keep grade points, quality points, and credit hours separate: grade points represent the letter-grade value; quality points are grade points multiplied by credits; and GPA is total quality points divided by total GPA credit hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are quality points the same as GPA points?

They are related but not identical. Grade points are assigned to a letter grade. Quality points are calculated by multiplying those grade points by course credit hours. GPA is the average calculated from total quality points and GPA credit hours.

Can I earn more quality points in a course with more credits?

Yes. If the grade-point value is the same, a course with more credit hours produces more quality points. For example, an A in a four-credit course earns 16 points on a 4.0 scale, while an A in a two-credit course earns 8.

Do F grades earn quality points?

On a common 4.0 scale, an F is worth 0.0 grade points, so it produces zero quality points. If the course is included in GPA, its credit hours may still be included in the GPA denominator.

How do I calculate my semester GPA from quality points?

Add the quality points from all courses included in the semester GPA, then divide by the total GPA credit hours for those courses.

Final Thoughts

Quality points help colleges account for both your course grades and the credit value of each class. To calculate them, convert each letter grade into grade points, multiply by the course's credit hours, and add the results. For an accurate GPA, follow your college's grading scale and rules about which courses count.
