How Does the US GPA System Work? A Complete Guide to the 4.0 Scale

August 14, 2026 · 6 min read

The US GPA system commonly uses a 4.0 scale, where letter grades are assigned grade-point values. At many colleges, semester GPA is calculated by weighting each course's grade-point value by its credit hours. Individual schools set their own grading policies, so exact grade-point values, percentage cutoffs, and calculation methods can vary from one institution to the next — this guide covers how the system generally works.

What Is GPA in the US?

GPA (Grade Point Average) is a single number that summarizes your academic performance by converting letter grades into numeric points and averaging them. GPA is commonly used to evaluate academic performance for purposes such as academic standing, scholarships, honors, and admissions, although the exact requirements vary by institution and program.

How Does the 4.0 GPA Scale Work?

Most US high schools and universities use some version of the following scale, but schools are not required to use identical grading scales. Some skip the +/− distinctions, some use a 4.3 top score for A+, and some weight grades differently by course level. Always check the specific grading policy published by the school or program you're interested in.

The percentage ranges below are illustrative rather than universal. US schools can assign different percentage cutoffs to letter grades, so students should use their institution's published grading scale when available.

Course Grade, Semester GPA, and Cumulative GPA

These three terms get used interchangeably, but they mean different things:

TermMeaning
Course gradeThe letter grade earned in a single course
Semester GPAThe GPA calculated from all courses taken in one semester
Cumulative GPAThe GPA calculated across the courses included in your cumulative GPA under your institution's rules

How Are Grade Points Calculated?

Each course contributes to your GPA based on its grade-point value and credit hours. The resulting product is often called quality points, although terminology can vary by institution.

Quality points = Grade-point value × Credit hours

A 4-credit course generates more quality points than a 1-credit course with the same letter grade, so it has a greater effect on GPA.

How Is Semester GPA Calculated?

Formula:

Semester GPA = Total quality points ÷ Total Credits Included in GPA

How Do Credit Hours Affect GPA?

At many US colleges, GPA is weighted by credit hours, meaning a 4-credit course has more influence on your GPA than a 1-credit course — grading policies do vary by institution, so this isn't universal. This means two students with the same letter grades can have different GPAs when their courses carry different credit weights. (See our upcoming guide on how credit hours affect your semester GPA for more detail.)

Weighted vs. Unweighted GPA

US GPA systems can be weighted or unweighted, particularly in high school. Unweighted GPA grades every course on the standard 4.0 scale regardless of difficulty; weighted GPA gives extra points for advanced coursework like AP, IB, or Honors classes, which is why some GPAs appear above 4.0. Because weighted GPA calculations differ by school, this article focuses on the standard 4.0 framework. What Is a Good Semester GPA?

There's no single GPA considered "good" for every US college, program, or scholarship — expectations vary by institution, major, and academic goal. (For a detailed breakdown by selectivity tier, see our upcoming guide: What Is a Good Semester GPA in a US College?)

Can International Grades Be Converted to a US GPA?

If you're applying from outside the US, there is no universal formula for converting your percentage, division, or CGPA into a US GPA. Different universities, credential evaluators, and even individual admissions offices convert international grades differently, and some recalculate a submitted transcript internally using their own methodology rather than accepting a self-reported conversion.

AACRAO — the professional association whose EDGE database many US registrars use to evaluate foreign grading scales — frames grade conversion as an area with multiple accepted approaches rather than one universal standard, and recommends checking the grading scale the issuing institution itself provides. Credential evaluation services such as WES typically evaluate transcripts course-by-course against each institution's own documented grading norms, rather than applying one fixed public conversion table per country.

If a GPA requirement is close for a school you're applying to, the most reliable approach is to check whether that university publishes its own country-specific equivalency guidance, or to ask its admissions office directly how it evaluates transcripts from your country and education board.

Why Percentage-to-GPA Formulas Can Be Misleading

You'll often see simplified formulas online, like dividing a percentage by 25 to get a GPA out of 4 (since 100 ÷ 25 = 4). This kind of formula is not a universal or official GPA conversion method. It ignores real factors:

  • Grading strictness varies enormously by country and even by individual school or board.
  • US letter-grade boundaries aren't evenly spaced at 25-point intervals (an A typically starts around 90–93%, not 75%).
  • Universities and credential evaluators may use country-specific grading information and different evaluation methodologies — not a single universal math formula.

Use simplified formulas only to get a rough personal sense of where you stand — never as the number you report on an application or expect a university to use.

FAQs

How does the US 4.0 GPA scale work?

Letter grades are assigned numeric point values, typically from 0.0 (F) to 4.0 (A), and those values are averaged — weighted by credit hours — to produce your GPA. Exact scales can vary slightly between schools.

How is the semester GPA calculated?

Multiply each course's GPA value by its credit hours to get quality points, add those together, then divide by total GPA credit hours for the semester.

Are US GPA scales identical at every university?

No. Most schools use some version of the 4.0 scale, but exact grade-point values, percentage cutoffs, and whether +/− grades are used can differ by institution.

Does GPA always use credit hours?

No. Many US colleges calculate GPA using credit hours or another form of course weighting, but policies vary by institution. Check your college's grading policy to see how GPA is calculated.

What's the difference between semester GPA and cumulative GPA?

Semester GPA covers only the courses taken in one term. Cumulative GPA applies the same weighted approach across the courses included in your institution's cumulative GPA calculation.

Can international grades be converted directly to a US GPA?

Not with one universal formula. Conversion methods vary by university and credential evaluator, and many recalculate a submitted transcript using their own methodology rather than accepting a self-reported number.
