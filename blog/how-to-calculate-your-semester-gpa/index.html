How to Calculate Semester GPA (Formula, Examples & Calculator)

October 31, 2026 · 7 min read

Your semester GPA measures your academic performance across the courses you take in one semester. You calculate it by converting each course grade into grade points, multiplying those grade points by the course's credit hours, adding the resulting quality points, and dividing by the total GPA credit hours. This guide walks you through each step with worked examples, including plus/minus grades and courses with different credit hours.

Quick formula: Semester GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total GPA Credit Hours (Quality Points = Grade Points × Credit Hours)

What Is a Semester GPA?

A semester GPA is a grade-point average that represents your academic performance during a specific semester. Unlike the grade for a single course, a semester GPA combines the grades from all the courses you complete in that academic term.

For example, a student might complete four courses during a semester:

CourseGradeCredit Hours
EnglishA3
MathematicsB4
BiologyA3
HistoryB3

Each letter grade is converted into grade points according to your institution's grading scale. Those grade points are then weighted by the number of credit hours.

Note: "Credit hours" are also called "credits" or "units" at some colleges and in some countries. They all mean the same thing here: the weight of a course.

If you are unfamiliar with how a semester grade itself is determined, see What Is a Semester Grade in the US Grading System?.

How Do You Calculate Your Semester GPA?

The general formula is:

Semester GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total GPA Credit Hours

Quality points are calculated by multiplying the grade points earned in a course by that course's credit hours.

Step 1: List All Your Courses

Start by listing every course included in your semester GPA.

CourseLetter GradeCredit Hours
EnglishA3
MathematicsB4
BiologyA3
HistoryB3

Include only courses that your institution counts toward the semester GPA. Pass/fail, audit, or withdrawn courses are often excluded.

Step 2: Convert Each Letter Grade Into Grade Points

Convert each letter grade into its grade-point value. A commonly used 4.0 scale (without plus/minus) is:

Letter GradeTypical Grade Points
A4.0
B3.0
C2.0
D1.0
F0.0

Many institutions also use plus and minus grades (see the next section). Because values differ between schools, always use the official grading scale published by your school or college.

Step 3: Multiply Grade Points by Credit Hours

CourseGradeGrade PointsCredit HoursQuality Points
EnglishA4.0312.0
MathematicsB3.0412.0
BiologyA4.0312.0
HistoryB3.039.0
Grade Points × Credit Hours = Quality Points

Step 4: Add Your Total Quality Points

12 + 12 + 12 + 9 = 45 quality points

Step 5: Add Your Total Credit Hours

3 + 4 + 3 + 3 = 13 credit hours

Step 6: Divide Quality Points by Credit Hours

45 ÷ 13 = 3.46

The student's semester GPA is approximately 3.46 on a 4.0 scale.

Rounding tip: Most schools report GPA to two decimal places (3.4615 becomes 3.46). Some round to three decimals, so check your institution's policy.

Why Do Credit Hours Matter When Calculating Semester GPA?

Credit hours determine how much weight each course carries. An A in a 4-credit course produces more quality points than an A in a 2-credit course:

  • 4.0 × 4 = 16 quality points
  • 4.0 × 2 = 8 quality points

How Do You Calculate Semester GPA With Different Credit Hours?

Consider a student taking three courses:

CourseGradeGrade PointsCreditsQuality Points
Course AA4.0312
Course BB3.0412
Course CC2.024
  • Total quality points: 12 + 12 + 4 = 28
  • Total credit hours: 3 + 4 + 2 = 9
  • Semester GPA: 28 ÷ 9 = 3.11

Can You Calculate Semester GPA Without Credit Hours?

Only if every course has the same number of credit hours. In that case, a simple average of grade points gives the same result as the weighted calculation.

When credit hours differ, a simple average gives the wrong answer. In the example above, averaging the grade points gives (4.0 + 3.0 + 2.0) ÷ 3 = 3.00, but the correct credit-weighted GPA is 3.11. Always use the credit hours listed by your institution.

For a detailed explanation, read How Do Credit Hours Affect Your Semester GPA?.

Can You Calculate Your Semester GPA From Percentages?

You can estimate a GPA from percentage grades, but the conversion depends on your institution's grading scale. For example, one school might use:

90–100%A4.0
80–89%B3.0
70–79%C2.0
60–69%D1.0
Below 60%F0.0

Another institution may use different cutoffs or plus/minus bands, so do not assume a given percentage always equals the same GPA. If you are unsure, check your school's grade conversion policy or ask your registrar's office. You can also read our guide on converting percentages to GPA.

Weighted vs. Unweighted GPA

Everything above describes an unweighted GPA, where an A is worth 4.0 regardless of course difficulty.

Some high schools also use a weighted GPA, which gives extra points for honors, AP, or IB courses. A common approach is:

Course LevelExtra PointsAn "A" Is Worth
Standard04.0
Honors+0.54.5
AP / IB+1.05.0

You calculate it the same way (quality points ÷ credit hours), but use the weighted grade-point value for each course. Weighting systems vary by school, so check your school's policy. Most colleges report an unweighted GPA.

What Is the Difference Between Semester GPA and Cumulative GPA?

A semester GPA covers coursework from one semester. A cumulative GPA combines applicable coursework from multiple semesters.

For example, a student could have:

  • Fall semester GPA: 3.2
  • Spring semester GPA: 3.6
  • Cumulative GPA: calculated from all applicable coursework across both semesters

The cumulative GPA is not necessarily the simple average of semester GPAs, because the number of credit hours can differ between semesters.

What Semester GPA Do You Need to Reach a Target Cumulative GPA?

Students often ask what GPA they need next semester to hit a goal. Use this formula:

Required semester GPA = (Target GPA × Total Credits After This Semester − Current Quality Points) ÷ Credits This Semester

Example: You have a 3.2 cumulative GPA over 30 credits and want a 3.4 cumulative GPA after taking 15 more credits.

  • Current quality points: 3.2 × 30 = 96
  • Total credits after this semester: 30 + 15 = 45
  • Quality points needed overall: 3.4 × 45 = 153
  • Quality points needed this semester: 153 − 96 = 57
  • Required semester GPA: 57 ÷ 15 = 3.8

If the result is above 4.0 (or your school's maximum), the target is not reachable in a single semester and would take more than one term.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a 3.0 a good semester GPA?

A 3.0 is roughly a B average on a 4.0 scale. Whether it is "good" depends on your goals. Many scholarships and programs require a minimum between 3.0 and 3.5, while academic probation often begins below 2.0. These thresholds vary by institution, so check your school's requirements.

Does an A always equal 4.0?

Not always. Many institutions assign 4.0 to an A, but scales differ, especially when plus and minus grades are used. Some schools award more than 4.0 for an A+ or use weighted scales.

Do failed courses affect semester GPA?

If a failed course is included in your institution's GPA calculation, its 0.0 grade points lower your semester GPA (the credits still count in the divisor). Treatment of failed, repeated, or excluded courses depends on institutional policy.

Are pass/fail courses included in semester GPA?

Usually not. Pass/fail courses are often excluded from GPA, though a fail may sometimes count. Check your institution's GPA policy.

Can I calculate my semester GPA before final grades are released?

Yes. You can estimate it using your expected grades, although the result may change once official grades are recorded.

How many decimal places should I use for GPA?

Two decimal places is standard (for example, 3.46), but some institutions report three. Follow your school's convention.

Key Takeaways

  • Semester GPA measures academic performance during one semester.
  • The basic formula is total quality points ÷ total GPA credit hours.
  • Quality points equal grade points multiplied by credit hours.
  • Courses with different credit hours must be weighted accordingly; a simple average can give the wrong result.
  • Plus/minus scales, percentage conversions, and weighted GPAs vary between institutions.
  • Semester GPA and cumulative GPA measure different academic periods.
  • A semester GPA calculator can save time and reduce errors.
