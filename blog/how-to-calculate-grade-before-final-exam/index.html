How to Calculate Your Grade Before the Final Exam

August 12, 2026 · 6 min read

If you know your current grade, how much your final exam is worth, and the grade you're aiming for, you can calculate exactly what score you need on your final — no guessing required. This guide gives you the formula, a calculator, and worked examples so you can find your number in under two minutes.

What Grade Do You Need on Your Final? (Quick Answer)

Your required final exam score depends on three things: your current grade, your final exam's weight, and your target overall grade. Plug those into the calculator below, or use the formula further down if you want to do it by hand.

What Information Do You Need?

Before you calculate anything, gather these three numbers:

  • Your current grade — your grade in the class before the final, based on everything graded so far
  • Your final exam's weight — how much the final counts toward your overall grade, as a percentage
  • Your target grade — the overall grade you want to end up with

How to Calculate the Required Final Exam Score

The Formula

Final Exam Score Needed = (Target Grade − (Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight))) ÷ Final Weight

Where: Target Grade = the overall grade you want. Current Grade = your grade in the class before the final. Final Weight = how much the final counts, as a decimal (e.g., 20% = 0.20).

Important: This formula assumes your current grade reflects all coursework completed before the final, and that the final exam is the only remaining weighted component. If you still have assignments, projects, or quizzes left to complete, factor those into your current grade first — otherwise your result won't be accurate.

Worked Example

Let's say: your current grade is an 85%, your final exam is worth 20% of your overall grade, and you want to end the semester with at least a 90% overall. (Whether 90% counts as an A−, A, or another letter grade depends on your school's grading scale.)

Plug it in:

Final Exam Score Needed = (90 − (85 × (1 − 0.20))) ÷ 0.20
Final Exam Score Needed = (90 − (85 × 0.80)) ÷ 0.20
Final Exam Score Needed = (90 − 68) ÷ 0.20
Final Exam Score Needed = 22 ÷ 0.20
Final Exam Score Needed = 110%

In this example, hitting a 90% overall isn't mathematically possible.

Now let's try a more realistic target — 80% overall:

Final Exam Score Needed = (80 − (85 × 0.80)) ÷ 0.20
Final Exam Score Needed = (80 − 68) ÷ 0.20
Final Exam Score Needed = 12 ÷ 0.20
Final Exam Score Needed = 60%

That's a much more manageable target.

What If You Need More Than 100%?

If the calculator says you need more than 100%, your target overall grade isn't mathematically achievable through the final exam alone. Try recalculating for a lower, still-acceptable target — a passing grade or your program's minimum instead of your ideal grade. Then check whether your course allows extra credit, grade replacement, or a drop-the-lowest-score policy that could close the gap.

How Does Final Exam Weight Affect Your Grade?

The higher your final exam's weight, the more it can move your grade — for better or worse. A final worth 40–50% means a strong performance can significantly boost a shaky semester, but it also means a rough exam day can hurt more.

How Do You Find Your Current Grade and Final Weight?

Your professor's syllabus almost always lists the exact breakdown — usually something like: Homework 20%, Midterms 30%, Participation 10%, Final Exam 40%. This is the number you'll plug into the calculator or formula above.

What If You Don't Know Your Current Grade?

Check your LMS gradebook, syllabus, or manually total up your completed points before using the final exam calculator. If your instructor weights assignments by category, make sure you're using your actual weighted current grade rather than just averaging the visible assignment scores — the two can differ quite a bit.

What If Your Class Uses Points Instead of Weighted Categories?

Not every course uses weighted categories. Some use points-based grading instead, where your grade is simply total points earned divided by total points possible.

For example, if you've earned 720 points out of 800 possible points so far and your final is worth 200 points, you can calculate your final course grade based on the score you earn on the final:

Final Course Grade = (Points Earned So Far + Final Exam Points) ÷ Total Possible Points × 100

If you score 150 out of 200 on the final:

(720 + 150) ÷ (800 + 200) × 100 = 87%

Common Scenarios

What Grade Do You Need to Pass?

A passing grade varies by school, course, and program. Some institutions may consider a D-range grade passing, while others require a C or higher, especially for major or prerequisite courses. Check your syllabus or student handbook for the exact passing threshold, then plug it in as your target grade.

What Grade Do You Need to Keep a B (or Target GPA)?

Set your target grade to the lower bound of your school's B range. For example, if your school considers 80% the minimum for a B, enter 80% as your target. This gives you the true minimum you need — hitting anything above that just adds a safety cushion.

What If Your Course Has Special Grade Requirements?

Nursing / Pre-Med Grade Cutoffs

Some nursing, pre-med, and other health science programs require students to meet a specific minimum course or exam grade, which may be higher than the general passing grade for the school. Falling short can mean retaking the entire course or, in some programs, dismissal. Confirm your exact program requirement — not just the general course syllabus — before calculating your target.

Scholarship & Dean's List GPA Requirements

If a scholarship or Dean's List spot depends on your GPA, first figure out what final letter grade you need in each course. Your school's GPA scale determines how those letter grades translate into GPA, so it's worth confirming the exact scale with your registrar.

How Do You Calculate Your Final Course Grade After the Exam?

The score you need on your final and your actual final course grade are two different things. Once you know (or have a guess at) your final exam score, you can project your overall course grade with this formula:

Final Course Grade = (Current Grade × (1 − Final Weight)) + (Final Exam Score × Final Weight)

For example, with an 85% current grade, a final worth 20%, and a 60% score on that final:

(85 × 0.80) + (60 × 0.20) = 80%

FAQs

Can my final exam grade lower my overall grade?

Yes — if you score lower on your final than your current class average, it can pull your overall grade down, especially if the final carries significant weight. This is exactly why calculating your target score in advance is worth doing before exam day, not after.

What if I need more than 100% on the final?

It means your target overall grade isn't mathematically achievable with the final exam alone. Recalculate for a lower target grade, and check whether your course offers extra credit, grade replacement, or other ways to make up the difference.

How do I calculate what I need on finals for multiple classes?

Calculate each course separately using its own current grade, final exam weight, and target grade. Because each course can have a different grading scale and weighting system, repeat the calculation for each class.

What's a passing final exam score?

This depends entirely on your school and course — some classes only require you to pass the final with a 50–60%, while others (especially in nursing or STEM programs) require 70%+ just on the exam itself, separate from your overall grade. Always confirm with your syllabus rather than assuming a standard cutoff.

Final Takeaway

Once you know your current grade and your final exam's weight, you can calculate exactly what you need — no more, no less — and study with a clear target instead of vague anxiety.
