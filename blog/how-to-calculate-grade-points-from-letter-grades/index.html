How to Calculate Grade Points From Letter Grades (US Scale)

August 16, 2026 · 6 min read

If you've ever looked at a US transcript and wondered how a "B+" turns into a number like 3.3, you're not alone. Converting letter grades into grade points is the foundation of how GPA works — and once you understand the conversion, the math behind GPA becomes simple.

This guide walks through exactly how letter grades map to grade points, how those points combine with credit hours, and how to calculate GPA step by step.

What Are Grade Points?

A grade point is the numerical value assigned to a letter grade. Instead of averaging letters directly (which isn't mathematically possible), schools convert each letter grade into a point value, then use those numbers to calculate an average — the GPA.

Many US schools and colleges use a 4.0 GPA scale, although the exact grade-point values and grading policies can vary by institution. On this scale:

  • A = 4.0 (the highest standard grade point)
  • F = 0.0 (failing)

Everything else falls somewhere in between.

The Common 4.0 Scale (Standard Letter Grades)

Here's the most widely used version of the 4.0 scale, without plus/minus modifiers:

This is the baseline scale many middle schools and some high schools use. However, many US high schools and colleges use a more granular version that includes plus/minus grades.

The Plus/Minus 4.0 Scale

The plus/minus scale adds finer distinctions between grades. On a commonly used plus/minus scale, grades such as B+ and B− are assigned separate grade-point values, often 3.3 and 2.7 respectively. However, the exact values depend on the institution.

Step-by-Step: Converting a Letter Grade to a Grade Point

Here's the basic process for converting any single letter grade:

  • Identify the letter grade you received (e.g., B+).
  • Find your school's official grading scale. This is usually listed in a course syllabus, student handbook, or registrar's website.
  • Match the letter grade to its corresponding grade point using that scale.
  • Record the grade point — this number is what gets used in GPA calculations, not the letter itself.

Example: A grade of B+ on the standard plus/minus scale converts to a grade point of 3.3.

That's it for a single grade. The more useful calculation — and the one most students actually need — is figuring out GPA across multiple classes, which requires factoring in credit hours.

How Do You Calculate Grade Points for One Course?

To calculate the grade points for one course, identify the letter grade, check your institution's official conversion scale, and use the corresponding numerical value. For example, if your school's scale assigns a B+ a value of 3.3, that course contributes 3.3 grade points before credit hours are considered.

This single-course conversion is the building block for everything that follows — once you know how to convert one grade, you can repeat the process across every course to calculate a full GPA.

Why Credit Hours Matter

Not all classes are weighted equally. A 4-credit lab science course counts more toward your GPA than a 1-credit seminar, even if you earned the same letter grade in both. That's why grade points alone aren't enough to calculate GPA — you need to combine them with credit hours (also called credit units or semester hours).

This is done using a value called quality points.

Quality Points = Grade Points × Credit Hours

The formula is straightforward:

Quality Points = Grade Point × Credit Hours for that course

This calculation is repeated for every course, and the results are added together before dividing by total credit hours.

Worked Example: Calculating Quality Points

Suppose you took four courses in a semester:

CourseLetter GradeGrade PointCredit HoursQuality Points
BiologyA4.044.0 × 4 = 16.0
EnglishB+3.333.3 × 3 = 9.9
HistoryA−3.733.7 × 3 = 11.1
StatisticsC+2.342.3 × 4 = 9.2

Each row shows how a letter grade becomes a grade point, and how that grade point turns into quality points once credit hours are factored in.

GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credit Hours

Once you have quality points for every course, calculating GPA is a matter of simple division:

GPA = (Sum of Quality Points) ÷ (Sum of Credit Hours)

Continuing the Worked Example

Using the table above:

  • Total Quality Points: 16.0 + 9.9 + 11.1 + 9.2 = 46.2
  • Total Credit Hours: 4 + 3 + 3 + 4 = 14
GPA = 46.2 ÷ 14 = 3.30

So this student's semester GPA is 3.30, even though none of their individual grades was exactly a 3.3 — the credit-hour weighting is what produces the final number.

A Second Worked Example (Uneven Credit Loads)

Credit hours don't need to be equal across courses for the math to work — that's the whole point of the system. Here's a case with more variation:

Notice that the 5-credit Chemistry course had a much bigger pull on the final GPA than the 1-credit Art elective, even though both grades were in the A range. This is why a single high grade in a heavy course can lift your GPA more than the same grade in a light course.

Grading Scales Vary by Institution — Always Check Your School's Policy

The values in this article reflect the most common conventions used across US high schools and colleges, but they are not universal. Before calculating your own GPA, keep in mind that:

  • Some schools do not use plus/minus grading at all, relying only on whole-letter grades.
  • Some cap A+ at 4.0; others allow it to exceed 4.0 (e.g., 4.3).
  • Some high schools use weighted scales that assign higher grade points for Honors, AP, or IB courses (e.g., an A in an AP class might be worth 5.0 instead of 4.0).
  • Some institutions use different point values for D+/D− or omit them entirely.
  • Community colleges, graduate programs, and professional schools may each apply their own variations.

Because of these differences, the only fully reliable source for your specific grade-point values is your school's official grading policy, typically found in the course catalog, student handbook, or registrar's office.

Summary

  • Many US schools use a 4.0 scale to convert letter grades into grade points, but the exact scale and grade-point values vary by institution.
  • Common plus/minus grading scales assign separate grade-point values to grades such as A−, B+, and B−, but the exact values vary by institution.
  • Quality Points = Grade Point × Credit Hours for each course.
  • GPA = Total Quality Points ÷ Total Credit Hours across all courses.
  • Grading and GPA scales differ by institution, so always confirm your school's specific conversion chart before calculating your own GPA.
