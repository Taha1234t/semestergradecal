Semester Grade vs. Final Grade: What's the Real Difference?

August 8, 2026 · 4 min read

If you’ve ever logged into your school portal and seen two different numbers — one labeled “Semester Grade” and another labeled “Final Grade” or “Current Grade” — you’re not imagining things, and you’re not doing anything wrong. These are genuinely two different numbers, and mixing them up is one of the most common reasons students get a surprise on report card day.

Here’s exactly what each one means and how they connect.

Quick answer

Your semester grade is the grade you’ve earned so far during that specific term, based on everything graded up to that point — homework, quizzes, tests, and participation. Your final grade is the number that actually gets recorded on your transcript, and it usually includes your semester grade plus your final exam score, combined using a specific weighting formula.

In short: your semester grade is a snapshot. Your final grade is the final word.

What “semester grade” actually includes

Your semester grade is a running average of everything that’s been graded during that term. Depending on your school, that typically includes:

  • Homework and classwork
  • Quizzes
  • Unit tests
  • Projects
  • Participation or attendance points (if your teacher grades that)

This number changes throughout the semester as new grades get added. If you check it in September, it’ll look different than if you check it in December — that’s normal, and it’s exactly why calculators exist that let you plug in your current numbers and see where you stand in real time.

What “final grade” actually means

This is where things get confusing, because “final grade” gets used two different ways depending on your school:

  1. Most commonly: Final grade = your semester grade combined with your final exam score, using a set formula (more on that below).
  2. Less commonly, especially in year-long courses: “Final grade” refers to your grade for the entire year, calculated by averaging both semesters together.

Your school’s grading policy or student handbook will tell you which version applies to you — but formula #1 is far more common in both high schools and colleges. For example, Carnegie Mellon University’s official grading policy clearly distinguishes between mid-semester grades and final grades, showing how one university formally defines these terms.

The formula most schools use

There’s no single universal formula, but here are the three most common setups you’ll run into:

Structure Formula
Standard Semester Grade (90%) + Final Exam (10%)
Heavier final Semester Grade (80%) + Final Exam (20%)
Even split First Quarter (45%) + Second Quarter (45%) + Final Exam (10%)

Some colleges skip the “quarter” breakdown entirely and just average every assignment across the whole semester, then blend in the final exam at the end. If you’re not sure which formula your class uses, check your syllabus — it’s almost always listed there.

Why this trips people up

The confusion usually comes down to one of three things:

  • Vague labeling — some gradebooks just say “Current Grade” without clarifying if that includes the final exam yet or not.
  • Different terms, same word — “final” can mean the final exam or the final result, and schools aren’t always consistent about which one they mean.
  • Timing — checking your grade before the final exam shows one number; checking after shows another. Same course, two very different numbers, same label.

What actually shows up on your transcript

Your transcript will almost always show the final grade — the number that includes your final exam. Your semester grade (the pre-final number) is usually just a working total that lives in your gradebook or portal, not something that gets permanently recorded anywhere official.

That’s exactly why so many students panic mid-semester: they see a number they think is locked in, when really it’s still very much in motion.

So which one should you actually be watching?

Both — but for different reasons. Your semester grade tells you where you stand right now, which is useful for figuring out how much effort you need to put in before finals. Your final grade is the number that actually counts on your transcript and GPA.
